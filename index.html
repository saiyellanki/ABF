Scope the system. Extract the real requirements. Walk the engineers through evidence.

ABF is a professional workbench for AI audits. Answer questions about intended purpose, role, and architecture. The engine returns official identifiers from NIST AI RMF 1.0, the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001 (titles), SR 11-7, OWASP LLM Top 10 2025, and NIST AI 600-1 — each with a citation, source URL, evidence to request, and interview script. It does not invent “ABF-CTL” controls, and it will not label a chatbot high-risk merely because it handles personal data.

Engagement path

  1. Record the system, owner, auditor role, and markets.
  2. Characterize architecture (classic ML, RAG, agentic, GPAI).
  3. Screen intended purpose against EU Annex III and Article 5 — with Art. 6(3) modelled.
  4. Track evidence, run walkthroughs by role, and export the workpaper.

Draft answers autosave in this browser. Import a previously exported JSON to resume fieldwork.

ABF is an audit-support tool, not legal advice, a notified-body opinion, or an ISO certification decision. Screening conclusions are labelled indicative and cite the rule applied. ISO/IEC 42001 “shall” statements are copyrighted and are not reproduced; titles only.

01

Accurate classification

EU high-risk follows Article 6 and Annex I/III. Profiling removes the 6(3) exception. SR 11-7 applies to models at supervised US banks, not every LLM.

02

Official IDs, not folklore

GOVERN 1.1, Article 14, A.8.4 (communication of incidents — not “system protection”). Every row has a URL you can open during fieldwork.
